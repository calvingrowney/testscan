# Interview guide: convenient-protein routines

## Opening and consent

> Thanks for talking with me. I’m doing a student branding research project about convenient protein foods. This is not a sales pitch and there are no right answers. I’ll summarize answers anonymously; you can skip any question or stop whenever you want. Is that okay?

Do not show the tuna concept until the final section. Take private, de-identified notes using a participant code.

## Questions about real behavior

1. Tell me about the last time you needed food after a workout, between classes, or on a busy day. What did you choose?
2. Walk me through the last week: when did you need something quick and filling? What did you buy, make, or skip?
3. What convenience foods do you buy most often? Where do you get them, and what do they cost?
4. What makes a protein option feel worth choosing? What makes it a hard no?
5. How much kitchen/fridge access do you usually have? How does that change what you buy?
6. What do you already think about tuna or other shelf-stable seafood? Tell me about the last time you ate it—or why you do not.
7. What feels annoying, awkward, expensive, unappealing, or uncertain about those foods?
8. If you regularly buy a case, bundle, or subscription of anything, what made you do that? If not, why not?
9. What brands in food, fitness, or snacks feel like they understand you? What do they do differently?

## Only after the behavior conversation: concept reaction

> I’m exploring a **concept** for a premium, shelf-stable tuna product sold in bulk online for people who want convenient protein. It is not a product for sale.

1. What is your immediate reaction?
2. What would make this relevant or irrelevant to your real routine?
3. What questions would you need answered before you trusted it?
4. What would you compare it to instead?
5. What does the idea get wrong about you or your friends?

## Close

> Is there anything I should have asked that would help me understand how you choose convenient foods?

Record: participant code, relevant segment, key quotes, observed tension, evidence against the concept, follow-up question. Never turn one comment into a universal insight.
