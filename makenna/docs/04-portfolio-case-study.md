# Portfolio case-study blueprint

## One-sentence project description

> I investigated whether a premium, bulk-buy tuna brand could make convenient protein feel more relevant to active college students and young adults. I directed a team of research, interview, strategy, critique, creative, and QA agents—but used real interviews and tests to make the decisions.

Only use this after updating it to match what was actually done.

## Recommended case-study flow

1. **The question** — The hypothesis and why Makenna found it interesting.
2. **The challenge** — Why tuna is culturally and commercially difficult: convenience versus perception, trust, routine, and differentiation.
3. **How I investigated** — Interview count, competition map, concept tests, and evidence standards. State sample limitations.
4. **The agent system** — A compact diagram or list of the eight roles, what each did, and what Makenna controlled.
5. **What I learned** — Three evidence-backed findings with de-identified quotes or carefully labeled survey results.
6. **Where AI was wrong** — At least two moments where a prediction was contradicted by research and the decision changed.
7. **The strategy** — Target segment, customer tension, positioning, proof boundaries, and alternatives rejected.
8. **Identity system** — Visual territory, naming rationale, logo, palette, typography, photography/art direction, and accessibility notes. Label all conceptual work.
9. **Experience and marketing** — Packaging, DTC flow, bundle logic, campaign concepts, and the test plan/results.
10. **Feasibility and responsibility** — Product/sourcing questions, claims boundaries, key risks, and what would require professional review before a real launch.
11. **Reflection** — What Makenna would test next and what the investigation taught her about branding.

## Proof-of-curiosity trail

Use this visual sequence in the page:

```text
QUESTION → INVESTIGATION → AGENT TASKS → EVIDENCE → HUMAN JUDGMENT
→ EXPERIMENT → RESULT → WHAT I LEARNED
```

The visual work matters, but it should support—not replace—the investigation.

## Asset checklist

- Hero image: one clear concept package/product moment, labeled "concept."
- 1 competitor map or category pattern graphic.
- 1 anonymized insight/quote graphic.
- 1 positioning card showing a rejected alternative.
- Identity system: logo, type, color, imagery, packaging, social example.
- 3–5 website/DTC screens.
- One page of process evidence: decision-log snippet, test result, and "AI was wrong" reflection.
- A transparent limitations/risk section.

## Publication checklist

- [ ] No participant identifiable information or raw private notes.
- [ ] All photos, fonts, icons, and mockups are owned, licensed, or properly credited.
- [ ] Conceptual elements are clearly described as conceptual.
- [ ] Claims and nutrition facts are either verified for the exact product or removed/marked placeholder.
- [ ] No false implication of a supplier, certification, customer, partnership, or product launch.
- [ ] Parent and Makenna have approved the final page.
