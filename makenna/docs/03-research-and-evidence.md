# Research, evidence, and claims protocol

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the strongest appropriate source before treating something as a fact.

1. **Primary evidence:** product labels, brand sites, government regulators, original studies, written supplier documentation, direct interview notes, own test results.
2. **Credible secondary evidence:** reputable industry reporting or market research, labeled as secondary.
3. **Hypotheses:** agent suggestions, social comments, anecdotes, and intuition. Useful for questions; not proof.

Every research note needs: source URL or file, access date, relevant quote/data point in Makenna's own words, what it supports, limitations, and next verification step.

## Interview ethics and privacy

- Invite only willing participants. Start with a simple consent statement: participation is optional, answers are for a student research project, results will be summarized anonymously, and they can skip anything.
- Avoid collecting health history, body measurements, diagnoses, payment details, or precise location. Do not interview minors without appropriate parent/school permission.
- Keep raw notes in a private folder; use first names/aliases or participant codes in public-facing work.
- Do not reward participants with an actual food product or make product-safety claims.

## Small-sample research language

For 12 interviews or a modest survey, say: "In this exploratory sample..." or "Participants we spoke with..." Never say "college students think" or claim a market size from a convenience sample.

## Food-specific risk checklist

Tuna is not a neutral canvas. Treat these as decision inputs, not post-launch fine print:

- **Species and mercury:** FDA distinguishes canned light tuna (including skipjack) as a "Best Choice" in its consumer fish advice; albacore/white and yellowfin tuna are "Good Choices," while bigeye is a "Choice to Avoid." The particular species and consumption context matter. [FDA fish advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish)
- **Consumption messaging:** FDA's Q&A notes that albacore typically contains about three times as much mercury as canned light tuna and recommends variety. Do not turn a broad advisory into a product-specific "safe to eat daily" claim. [FDA Q&A](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/questions-answers-fdaepa-advice-about-eating-fish-those-who-might-become-or-are-pregnant-or)
- **Product and label claims:** Terms that characterize nutrient levels—such as "high"—fall within FDA nutrient-content-claim rules. An accurate nutrient quantity may still create an implied nutrient-level claim depending on wording and context. [FDA label claims](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/label-claims-conventional-foods-and-dietary-supplements)
- **Advertising:** Food health-related claims must be truthful, not misleading, and supported before advertising; implied claims count too. [FTC health-products guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)
- **Manufacturing/importing:** Seafood offered in U.S. interstate commerce is subject to seafood HACCP and sanitation requirements; importing can add verification obligations. [FDA seafood HACCP information](https://www.fda.gov/food/hazard-analysis-critical-control-point-haccp/lists-foreign-processors-fish-and-fishery-products-approved-their-governments)

These sources guide research only. Before any commercial action, engage appropriate food-safety, regulatory/labeling, legal, supply-chain, insurance, tax, and financial professionals.

## Claim discipline

The words below are not interchangeable. Flag them in the claim register before using them:

- **Nutrition:** high protein, protein-packed, lean, low sodium, omega-3 rich
- **Performance/health:** fuels workouts, recovery, muscle, clean energy, healthy
- **Safety:** low mercury, safe daily, tested
- **Sourcing/environment:** sustainable, responsibly caught, traceable, wild, ocean-friendly
- **Comparative/value:** more protein, cleaner, better, best, less mercury

For each, ask: What would a reasonable buyer think this means? Does the exact finished product have supporting evidence? Does the evidence cover the specific claim, serving size, species, source, and execution? Who has signed it off?

## Research log checklist

Before closing any phase, confirm:

- [ ] Sources and dates are saved.
- [ ] Facts, inferences, and ideas are labeled separately.
- [ ] Contradictory evidence is recorded.
- [ ] Every customer quote is anonymized or consented.
- [ ] Every claim is in the claim register or removed.
- [ ] Makenna's judgment and the next question are written down.
